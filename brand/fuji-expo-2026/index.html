01

The brief & the roots

FUJI EXPO 2026 is a flagship gathering for the Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu community — competition, gear, seminars and brand activations under one roof. The identity has to work loud at arena scale, hold up small on a lanyard, and feel current to a young, style-literate audience that lives on Instagram and TikTok.

The name gives us our raw material. Fuji carries a clean set of associations — the mountain, the ascent, the rising sun, and the Japanese martial heritage underneath the Brazilian sport. The strongest routes mine that world rather than defaulting to generic “extreme sports” tropes.

Crucially, EXPO isn’t landing on a blank page. It joins an established family — the World Championships and Pan American Championships already run a confident house style. Our job is to sit unmistakably inside that family while carving EXPO its own space: a competition is a fight card, but an expo is a gathering — gear, seminars, activations, community — so the identity can be broader and more welcoming without losing the edge.

02

What’s driving sports graphics in 2026

Six currents shaping combat-sports and event design right now. Each of our three routes is anchored in a different cluster of these — so the client is choosing a considered position, not a mood.

T/01

Oversized condensed type

Ultra-bold, tightly-tracked condensed caps used as the primary graphic element — type is the artwork. Loud, kinetic, unmistakably athletic. The dominant register in MMA, running and streetwear.

Anchors → Route 01 · Summit
T/02

Chrome & acid gradients

The Y2K/techwear revival: liquid-chrome lettering, mesh gradients and “digital sunrise” colour ramps. Reads future-facing and screen-native — built for reels and LED walls.

Anchors → Route 02 · Meridian
T/03

Heritage-modern craft

Traditional marks — crests, seals, brush ink — rebuilt with contemporary precision. Signals authenticity and premium. Strong fit for a sport with deep lineage and belt culture.

Anchors → Route 03 · Crest
T/04

Bilingual lockups

Japanese and Latin scripts set together as one composition — 富士 beside FUJI. Adds texture, authenticity and instant global legibility. Now standard in fight-wear and fashion.

Used across → all three routes
T/05

Modular, motion-ready systems

Identities designed as kits — a grid, a set of marks, a rule for how they animate — so they scale from a ticket to a 20-metre banner and move natively in video.

Baked into → every system
T/06

Tactile print & finish

Foil, emboss, riso texture and heavyweight stock pushing back against flat digital. Where it matters most for us: the medal and premium credential — objects people keep.

Peaks on → medal & credentials
03

Three routes to take to the client

Each route is a complete world: primary logo lockup, key-art poster, colour, type — then the same system carried onto a medal, event signage and a social tile. All three keep the FUJI badge and house colours; they differ in how far they push the current championship look — Summit stays closest, Crest elevates it, Meridian departs furthest.

R/01

SUMMIT

House evolution · type-first

The idea

The house look, dialled up. This is the closest evolution of the current championship style — same black ground, FUJI red, gold badge and hex texture — but we retire the busy esports chevrons for one decisive move: type is the mountain. A single ultra-bold condensed wordmark with a triangular apex that doubles as peak and directional arrow — the ascent.

The lowest-risk route: it will read instantly as FUJI to anyone who knows the World Championships, while feeling sharper and more grown-up. Loud, athletic, and it crops endlessly for social.

R/02

MERIDIAN

Techwear · neo-Tokyo

The idea

A digital sunrise over the mountain. The badge and red still anchor it, but this route makes the biggest leap from the current look — a gradient sun-disc dissolving into scanlines, liquid-chrome type and a bilingual 富士 / FUJI lockup. It gives EXPO its own screen-native world, distinct from the competition brands.

The boldest, most “internet” option. Highest energy on video; needs careful print management so the chrome and gradients hold on merch and signage.

R/03

CREST

Heritage mon · craft

The idea

A family crest for the sport. This route draws out the gold and the mountain already living in the FUJI badge and makes them the whole story. A circular mon — the Japanese crest form — encloses Mount Fuji rising from a stylised wave, drawn with the precision of a coin die. Gold on deep indigo, a serif wordmark, and a real Japanese title lockup. Restrained, lineage-aware, premium.

The most timeless and the most ownable — it won’t date. Reads as heritage and quality; the natural home for foil, emboss and a medal people genuinely want to keep.

Our recommendation

Lead with Crest — with Summit’s energy in the toolkit.

All three build from the existing FUJI badge and house colours, so none of them abandon the equity the Championships have already earned. Crest goes furthest in elevating it — an ownable, lineage-true mark that draws out the gold and mountain already in the logo and will still look right in 2030. It’s also the strongest home for the medal, the one object attendees keep. Its risk is feeling formal, so we’d pair it with Summit — the close evolution of today’s championship look — as the kinetic “hype layer” for social, apparel and reels. That gives the client one dignified anchor plus a loud, motion-ready campaign voice: heritage where it earns trust, energy where it drives attendance. Meridian is the boldest swing — hold it if the client wants EXPO to break away and own a young, online-first space distinct from the competition brands.
