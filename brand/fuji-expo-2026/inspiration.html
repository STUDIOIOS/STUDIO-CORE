01

How to use this board

Six territories of existing work that bear on FUJI EXPO. Each plate gives a visual cue, the real references (linked — open them to see the actual artwork) and a short “steal this” list of exactly what we’d carry into the design.

The tiles on each plate are Studio IOS interpretations — original, abstract motifs drawn to signal the territory. They are not the referenced artworks, which remain the property of their creators; use the links to view the originals. This keeps the board shareable and copyright-clean.

02

The reference board

T/01

Combat-sports event identity

How the majors build a fight-night look: a strong hero lockup, high-contrast type, and a modular system that scales from ticket to arena. Note ADCC’s no-gi grappling world specifically — closest to FUJI’s audience.

References

ADCC ADCC · IG ONE Championship UFC key art · Behance

Steal this

  • One dominant hero lockup, everything else subordinate
  • Black ground + single hot colour = instant fight energy
  • A system, not a poster — build for every touchpoint
T/02

Japanese heritage & kamon

The 1,000-year kamon (family-crest) tradition — motifs balanced with compass-precision inside a circle — and Asao Tokolo’s Tokyo 2020 harmonised chequered emblem, built from modular ichimatsu shapes. This is the deepest, most ownable well for a brand literally named after a Japanese mountain.

References

Kamon: original logos of Japan Kamon artisans Tokyo 2020 emblem Asao Tokolo

Steal this

  • Build the mark inside a circle with true geometric rigour
  • Modular units → many layouts from one kit (unity in diversity)
  • Restraint reads as premium; indigo + off-white as a base
T/03

Grappling apparel & drop culture

BJJ’s own design economy: Shoyoroll turned gis into limited “batch” collectibles; Hyperfly and the RVCA collabs push bold streetwear crossover. Merch is where an expo makes memories (and money) — treat drops, patches and heritage detailing as first-class.

References

Shoyoroll (batch culture) Hyperfly Sports-luxe · Behance

Steal this

  • Numbered / dated “drop” framing → collectible EXPO ’26 merch
  • Woven-patch & stitch detailing signals craft and authenticity
  • A crossover capsule with the streetwear-literate audience
T/04

Medal & trophy craft

Paris 2024 set the modern bar: Chaumet built the medal around a hexagon, a real material story (18g of Eiffel Tower iron), Clous de Paris hobnail setting and embossed radiating lines. The lesson for FUJI: a medal is a keepsake — give it geometry, a story and tactile finish.

References

Paris 2024 medals Dezeen breakdown Tokyo podiums

Steal this

  • Anchor the medal to one geometry — a Fuji hexagon / summit
  • Embossed radiating lines > flat engraving for “shine”
  • Design the ribbon as hard as the disc; consider a material story
T/05

Esports & broadcast systems

VALORANT Champions (BUCK / Worship Studio) is the reference for a modular identity across many sub-events, one animation language, and the “technical depth” look — HUD brackets, grids, timestamps. Directly relevant to an expo with many zones, stages and a live broadcast.

References

VALORANT Champions · BUCK VCT23 · Worship Studio Sports graphics trends

Steal this

  • One core mark + colour-coded sub-brands per zone/stage
  • Technical overlays (brackets, grids, timecodes) = credibility
  • Define the motion language up front — it’s a video-first event
T/06

2026 graphic currents

Where the wider field is heading this year: neon & glow, bold high-contrast type, “technical depth” overlays, the chrome/gradient revival, and sports-luxe (athleisure-meets-luxury) powered by the season’s big tournaments. Useful for keeping EXPO current — used deliberately, not all at once.

References

It’s Nice That · 2026 ScoreVision · sports Envato · trends

Steal this

  • Pick one trend to lead with — layering all of them dates fast
  • Neon/glow & chrome live best on screen & LED, not print
  • “Sports-luxe” = the bridge between the fight world and premium
