LICENSED MERCHANDISE FOR PFL AND MVP

PFL and MVP merge with the licensing already built. CAA Sports Licensing holds the trademark rights across categories, Takedown covers performance and lifestyle apparel, and the MMA debut in May pulled 12.4 million viewers on Netflix.

What is missing is the artwork. One commission per card can carry a tee, a print, a trading card, a collectible and a figure. The UFC has been running that model for years, and licensed sport is now the fastest growing category in the licensing industry.

01  THE MODEL

One Commission,
Every Category

The UFC hands a marquee card to an artist and runs the result across the whole shop. An event tee on the night. A framed print at $199.99. Numbered trading cards in runs of a few hundred. Canvas editions pressed onto used Octagon canvas, ten of each. Designer art toys built with Stancé and stylised by Tracy Tubera.

The same drawing runs from an event tee to a $199.99 framed print, and carries into categories apparel cannot reach on its own.

02  THE PROOF

Artwork Moves
Merchandise

UFC 300 sold more merchandise than any arena event in the company's history. The card carried its own Artist Series poster, and the shop carried the tee alongside it.

When MLB gave Takashi Murakami the Tokyo Series, the collection sold out on the Fanatics app inside the hour, most of it gone in fifteen minutes, after 100,000 fans downloaded the app to get at it. The event took $40 million in gear and cards, a Fanatics record. Artwork is what turns a fan watching into a fan buying.

03  THE STUDIO

What We
Build

We make the artwork and the rules that hold it together. For Fuji we delivered the brand guide and the style guide, with fighter artwork researched properly, like the Rickson Gracie graphic drawn from his own fight material.

For Scramble and Phalanx we run the seasonal trend work and turn it into collections. Everything is built in vector so it re-colours cleanly, drops into a licensee pack, and scales from a fight kit to a card back without being redrawn.
