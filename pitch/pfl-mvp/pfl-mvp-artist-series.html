01  THE PRECEDENT

UFC Artist Series

The UFC commissions original artwork for its marquee cards and releases it as Artist Series prints, tees and collectibles, extending into designer art toys with Stancé and illustrator Tracy Tubera. The economics sit in scarcity and price: framed Artist Series prints retail at $199.99, numbered runs land in the low hundreds, and canvas editions pressed onto used Octagon canvas are made in tens. Each card becomes a dated, collectable drop rather than a restock of the primary logo.

02  THE DEMAND

Artist Led Product Sells

MLB ran the same play at scale. Takashi Murakami artwork across jerseys, hoodies and caps sold out on the Fanatics app in under an hour, most of it inside 15 minutes, after more than 100,000 fans downloaded the app to reach it. The Tokyo Series took $40 million in fan gear and trading cards, the best selling event on Fanatics' record.

Formula 1 shows the pattern holding in apparel. Fashion led collaborations have run to roughly 80 per cent sold through at some retailers, apparel sponsorship spend is up 75 per cent in two years, and other revenue covering licensing, merchandising and hospitality rose 20 per cent to $787 million in 2025.

03  THE HEADROOM

Combat Sports Under Trades

UFC consumer products and licensing was $55.0 million in 2024, around 4 per cent of UFC revenue and down on the year before. WWE, under the same owner and the same commercial machine, took $136.4 million and grew 23 per cent on a programme built around design, collaboration and repeat drops. ONE Championship has chased the same ground since 2018 with SBTG and Shoyoroll. Combat sports has the appetite. What it lacks is the creative system feeding the licensees.

04  THE STUDIO

Proven in Sport and Combat

For Fuji we delivered the brand guide and style guide, including fighter led artwork grounded in research such as the Rickson Gracie graphic built from his own fight material. For ESPN we translated broadcast storytelling into a fashion first style guide, reframing a sports broadcaster as a lifestyle brand embedded in the everyday culture of sport. The same work runs through Chelsea, Ferrari, LA28 and the FIA World Endurance Championship, alongside seasonal trend reporting for Scramble and Phalanx.
