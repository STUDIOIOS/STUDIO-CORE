# STUDIO IOS Voice — Examples

Canonical voice samples plus worked before/after rewrites for every deck page type. Use these as calibration: match this rhythm and specificity when writing new copy.

---

## Part 1 — Canonical samples (real Studio IOS copy)

These are genuine Studio IOS lines. Read them before writing to tune to the voice. They're from client outreach, but the DNA is identical to deck copy: decision-led, evidence-first, no fluff, UK spelling.

> "The style-guide function shifted from compliance document to commercial tool. Licensees started using it as creative input, not just brand-protection input. Range coherence improved measurably across categories."

> "FUJI Sports: 17% YoY uplift on the season we directed. Trend signals locked 4 months ahead of the brief; collection structured around 3 graphic anchors instead of 9 disconnected ones."

> "Phalanx: preorder revenue covered inventory + studio fees before launch. Achieved by tightening the graphic language to a single 'buyable identity' the audience recognised instantly."

> "Manufacturers can produce; they can't art-direct. The result is collections that ship on time but feel reactive instead of anticipatory."

> "Distinctive creative is the only thing that lets a £55 rashguard actually sell at £55."

What to notice:
- Decision or claim first, proof immediately after.
- Numbers are concrete and attributed to real work.
- Contrast is used to sharpen ("compliance document → commercial tool"; "produce vs art-direct").
- Not one hype adjective. The confidence comes from specificity.
- Sentence lengths vary; nothing is sing-song.

## Part 2 — Worked rewrites by page type

Each shows a generic/AI draft and the Studio IOS rewrite for the field the `studio-ios-pdf` skill expects.

### `intro` — concept overview body

**❌ Generic / AI:**
> In today's competitive landscape, this collection represents a bold new chapter for the brand. Leveraging a vibrant palette and striking graphics, it delivers a dynamic visual identity that elevates the brand to the next level, showcasing its commitment to innovation and quality.

**✅ Studio IOS:**
> The collection is built around a single mark — the reworked academy crest — applied the same way on every piece. Colour is held to charcoal and bone so the range reads as one family on the shelf. The brief asked for product that could price up; the answer is a graphic system recognisable enough to justify it. Everything downstream, from the badge to the hem detail, resolves off those two decisions.

Why it works: opens on the decision, names the brief's actual ask, proves with specifics (two colours, one mark), closes on outcome. One dash, earning its place.

---

### `divider` — section + accent_word

**❌ Generic:** section `"OUR CREATIVE JOURNEY"`, accent_word `"DYNAMIC"`

**✅ Studio IOS:** section `"GRAPHIC ASSETS"`, subsection `"CONCEPT BOARDS"`, accent_word `"EXPLORATORY"`

Section titles are structural nouns. The accent word is a single earned word that flags the register of the section (exploratory, wildcard, core), never a mood adjective.

---

### `asset` — item caption

**❌ Generic / AI:**
> A stunning and versatile badge design that beautifully captures the essence of the brand.

**✅ Studio IOS:**
> Woven-edge badge, single colour — built to survive hem embroidery and still read at three metres.

The caption is a creative instruction the client can judge: what it is, and the constraint it's designed around. Specific enough that the artwork could be briefed straight from it.

More on-brand asset captions:
> "Primary lockup, stacked — for the small-scale applications where the horizontal mark loses legibility."
> "Tonal colourway, charcoal on charcoal — for the pieces where the graphic should register on touch, not from across the room."

---

### `feature` — application description (2–3 lines)

**❌ Generic / AI:**
> This striking application showcases the badge in a dynamic new context, seamlessly blending form and function to create a truly premium product experience that resonates with the modern consumer.

**✅ Studio IOS:**
> The woven badge sits at the lower-front hem, off-centre, where it reads on the body without competing with the chest print. Single-colour thread on tonal ground keeps it premium-quiet rather than loud. Final board takes it onto the core tee in both colourways.

Lead sentence = the application decision (placement + why). Middle = the specific (material, treatment). Final = the outcome / next board. This is where rationale sells; earn it.

---

### `product` — description line

**❌ Generic:**
> A beautifully crafted tee featuring premium details throughout.

**✅ Studio IOS:**
> `RIGHT-CHEST LOGO / WOVEN HEM BADGE / ACCENT MARK`

Product descriptions are tight spec lines. Slash-separated placements, caps, no adjectives. The garment sells itself; the line just says where the graphics land.

---

### `cover` — project / collection lockup

Nouns, not sentences. Set language, tight and typographic.

> project: `PHALANX` · collection: `CORE SYSTEM 26` · stage: `001`

Don't write a tagline here. The cover is a lockup, not a headline.

## Part 3 — The rewrite move, generalised

Almost every fix is the same motion: **an adjective is hiding a missing specific — go find the specific.**

| Vague claim | Specific that replaces it |
|---|---|
| "bold graphics" | "one mark, repeated at three scales" |
| "premium feel" | "single-colour tonal embroidery, no contrast thread" |
| "versatile system" | "scales from a 15mm woven badge to a 40cm back print" |
| "cohesive collection" | "every piece traces to the same crest and two colours" |
| "eye-catching" | "reads at three metres" |
| "timeless design" | "built to carry across four collections, not one drop" |

When you can't find the specific, the copy isn't ready — ask for the missing creative detail rather than papering it with an adjective.
